# Chemistry with Mummy: storyboard

Every scene follows the same five beats: **watch** (animation with narration), **watch** (the idea explained), **predict** (guess first), **try it** (hands-on task), **remember** (summary), then a 3 or 4 question quiz. A final round and a cheat sheet finish the explainer.

## Act 1: Atoms and the periodic table

| # | Scene | Big idea | Guess first | Hands-on task |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | Inside the atom | Protons, neutrons and electrons | A neutral atom has 6 protons. How many electrons does it have? | Challenge: build a neutral atom of carbon (6 protons). |
| 1.2 | Atomic number and mass number | Neutrons = mass number − atomic number | Chlorine has mass number 35 and atomic number 17. How many neutrons? | Work out the particles in a neutral atom of the element shown. |
| 1.3 | Electrons in shells | Shells fill 2, 8, 8 | What is the electronic configuration of magnesium (12 electrons)? | Move the slider to add electrons. |
| 1.4 | The periodic table | Groups are columns, periods are rows | An element has configuration 2,8,6. Which group and period is it in? | Click the element in Group 2, Period 3. |
| 1.5 | Group 1: the alkali metals | Reactivity increases down the group | Rubidium is below potassium in Group 1. How will it react with water? | Choose a metal, then drop it into the water. |
| 1.6 | Halogens and noble gases | Group 7 gets less reactive down; Group 0 is unreactive | Astatine is below iodine in Group 7. What do you expect at room temperature? | Tap a fact, then tap its group. |

## Act 2: Bonding and structure of materials

| # | Scene | Big idea | Guess first | Hands-on task |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | Why do atoms bond? | To get a full outer shell | Sodium has configuration 2,8,1. How can it get a full outer shell most easily? | Does the atom lose, gain or share electrons? |
| 2.2 | Ionic bonding | Electrons transfer; opposite charges attract | What is the charge on a magnesium ion? | Choose a metal and a non-metal, then press Make the compound. |
| 2.3 | Ionic compounds: structure and properties | Giant lattice; conducts only when molten or dissolved | Does solid sodium chloride conduct electricity? | Test each of the three states. |
| 2.4 | Covalent bonding | Non-metal atoms share pairs of electrons | How many covalent bonds does the carbon atom form in methane, CH₄? | Look at four different molecules. |
| 2.5 | Simple molecular substances | Weak forces between molecules, so low melting and boiling points | When water boils, what is broken? | Challenge: find a temperature where methane is a gas, water is a liquid and iodine is still a solid. |
| 2.6 | Giant covalent structures | Diamond, graphite and silicon dioxide | Why can graphite conduct electricity? | Choose a material, then run each test. |
| 2.7 | Which structure is it? | Use properties to decide: ionic, simple molecular or giant covalent | A substance melts at −90 °C and does not conduct electricity. What is it most likely to be? | Substance 1 of 4 |

| # | Scene | Big idea | Guess first | Hands-on task |
|---|---|---|---|---|
